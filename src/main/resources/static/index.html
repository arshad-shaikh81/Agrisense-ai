Background

Why the current way of watering fields falls short

Most small and mid-sized farms still rely on the calendar or a farmer's eye, not on what the soil is actually saying.

Fixed schedules

Traditional irrigation

Farmers water on a fixed calendar or by manual judgement, regardless of what the soil actually needs that day.

Single outlet, whole field

Uneven moisture distribution

Flood and manual methods soak some patches while leaving others dry, since a single outlet can't reflect field variation.

Soil type, slope, shade

Need for zone-wise monitoring

Different zones of the same field differ in soil type, slope and shade, so one blanket schedule can't fit every zone.

Water, power, roots

Need to reduce unnecessary watering

Over-irrigation wastes water and electricity, leaches nutrients, and can damage roots — a growing concern as water tables fall.

Problem statement

Most small and mid-sized farms still irrigate on fixed schedules or manual inspection, with no continuous, zone-wise visibility into real soil-moisture and micro-climate conditions — leading to over- or under-watering, wasted water and power, and reduced crop yield, with no low-cost, easy-to-deploy system to close this gap.

No zone-wise, real-time soil data at the root level.

Significant, avoidable water and energy wastage.

No predictive decision-making — only reactive watering.

Project updates

Bill of materials

What we actually built this with

A deliberately low-cost stack — off-the-shelf sensors and boards, chosen so any small farm could replicate the setup without specialised equipment. Tap a row for a closer look.

Sensing & control

Arduino Uno × 1 Main controller — reads the sensor array and drives the relay/pump.
ESP32 NodeMCU dev board × 1 Connects to Wi‑Fi and sends pump-status alerts to the Telegram bot.
Soil moisture sensor (YL‑69 probe + comparator module) × 1 per zone Two-pronged probe reads root-zone moisture; the LM393 board outputs an analog/digital signal.
1‑channel relay module × 1 Switches the pump on and off under microcontroller control.

Irrigation hardware

Mini submersible water pump × 1 Draws water from the reservoir and delivers it to the zone on demand.
Silicone delivery tubing 1 length Carries water from the pump outlet to the irrigation point.

Power

Mini solar panel × 1 Keeps the system field-deployable without mains power.
9V battery + snap connector × 2 Backup/primary power for the controller and pump circuit.
Jumper wires (M‑M / M‑F) 1 set Wiring between sensors, relay, and controller boards.
